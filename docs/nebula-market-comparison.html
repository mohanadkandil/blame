Nebula vs Your Current Build (as of May 12, 2026)

Based on your current project (/Users/mohannedkandil/conductor/workspaces/blame/madison/README.md and docs/MVP_FEATURES.md), you are building a Mac-native coding-agent orchestrator (local repos, git worktrees, Elixir supervision, diff-first review).

What Nebula is doing

  • Nebula is positioning itself as a broad “AI workforce” platform, not just a coding tool.
  • It is cloud-first and automation-first: agents, channels, triggers, integrations, messaging delivery.
  • It is optimized for business workflows (email, Slack, CRM, social, reporting, ops automation), with low setup and no-code onboarding.
  • It has strong platform primitives: persistent memory, model selection across many models, integrations, and scheduled/event-driven execution.
  • Its pricing model signals usage economics: subscription + model credits + infrastructure surcharge.

What the market looks like

  • Demand is real and growing fast: Gartner projects large enterprise-app adoption of task-specific agents by 2026.
  • Hype is also high: Gartner also projects a large share of agentic projects will be canceled by 2027 due to cost/value/risk issues.
  • The market is entering consolidation: Gartner says supply currently exceeds demand and expects correction.
  • Incumbents are all in: Salesforce (Agentforce), Microsoft (Foundry Agent Service), Google (Vertex AI Agent Builder), which increases competition in horizontal “agent platform” space.

How Nebula differs from your current direction

Dimension Nebula Your Current Build
Core user Cross-functional teams (ops, sales, marketing, support, founders) Software engineers managing coding agents
Execution model Cloud, always-on virtual device + triggers Local Mac process orchestration + git worktrees
Primary surface Conversational automation platform Diff/review/merge workflow for code changes
Main value General productivity and workflow automation Reliable multi-agent software delivery
Competitive pressure Competes with broad enterprise agent suites Competes with coding orchestrators and dev tooling stacks

Best opportunity for you

  1. Do not compete with Nebula on breadth. Compete on depth for engineering reliability.
  2. Double down on your stated wedge: verification + attribution + FinOps for code agents.
  3. Become the “control plane for shipping safe agent code,” where teams can prove quality before merge.
  4. Add hybrid capability later (local-first plus optional cloud burst) to stay aligned with the market shift (for example, Conductor Cloud early access).

Positioning line you can use

“Nebula is building the AI workforce platform for business workflows; we are building the reliability and governance layer for engineering teams shipping code with multiple agents.”
